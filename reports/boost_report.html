Paid vs Organic Separation

Tom asked: if we take the views on the platform and subtract one paid metric from Andrew's paid table, do we get organic views? We tested every paid metric against opt-in views, which are organic only. One metric matches, but the subtraction still fails for single posts. A different method works. Part 2 shows how well we can tell which posts are paid, with every standard classification output.

Answer
  1. Impressions is the paid metric that matches. Instagram: public − opt-in = 1.07× paid Instagram-placement impressions (typical; middle half 1.01×–1.15×; 146 boosted posts after the ads ended). Facebook: SocAPI's extra plays = 1.08× paid Facebook-placement video plays (20 posts). The 2-second, 3-second, ThruPlay and percent-watched counts are 5.25× to 110× too small.
  2. The subtraction still fails for one post. Paid is typically 94% of public views on a boosted post, so a small paid error becomes a large organic error. Best case: typical error 70%, 31% of posts within ±25%, 11% negative. On Andrew's 10 tagged posts while the ads ran, 47% of days give negative organic.
  3. Use the pre-boost read × organic growth instead. Typical error 11.5%; 77% of posts within ±25%; never negative (117 boosted Instagram posts vs opt-in truth). For a 10-post campaign total, 95% of totals land within ±25% (subtraction: 59%).
  4. Andrew's tag is the best proof of paid. Our ad-name rule finds 21 of 21 tagged Meta posts. The TikTok Spark link misses all 3 tagged TikTok campaign posts. The daily table puts the tag first.
  5. Where no paid record exists, the model works. Locked test of later posts: TikTok F1 0.93, ROC AUC 0.96; Instagram F1 0.77, ROC AUC 0.89, precision 0.92. Tom's hand rule: F1 0.84 and 0.54.
Ask Andrew and the paid team for
  1. The post ID or URL on every boosted ad, on every platform. The match on his tag is exact.
  2. At least 7 days, best 14, between publish and the first boost. A pre-boost read on day 14 or later gives 8.6% typical error (65 posts, 91% within ±25%). A read before day 7 gives 30%.
  3. A dark post, or a creator opt-in, when a boost must start on day 0. Today 12% of boosted Instagram posts and 24% of boosted TikTok posts cannot be separated.
The caveat that can change the decision

TikTok organic is UNVERIFIED after a boost. TikTok opt-in views include Spark Ad views, so we have no organic truth there. The method passes a back-test on unboosted TikTok posts (day-14 read: 9% typical error), but not on boosted ones. Label TikTok organic numbers as estimates until one dark-post or holdout test exists.

3. The method that works: pre-boost read × organic growth

4. TikTok and YouTube

  • TikTok: the public lift after a boost is 1.17× the paid video plays (middle half 1.08×–1.47×; impressions 1.15×; 512 posts). Paid is typically 83% of public views.
  • TikTok opt-in views equal public views (typical ratio 1.000, 142 posts), so opt-in includes Spark Ad views. There is no organic truth on TikTok after a boost.
  • On TikTok, subtraction gives a typical 5.19× the curve estimate (middle half 1.80×–52×). We cannot check which is right on TikTok. On Instagram, where we can check, the curve wins.
  • YouTube: the warehouse has no ad-to-video link (0 of 34,687 posts match), so the math cannot be tested.

Part 2 · Andrew's tag as the gold check

Andrew's tag confirms our links on Meta, and finds TikTok boosts we missed

Since 2026-09-15 the paid team puts the organic post ID in PAID_MEDIA_UNIFIED.ext_p3_organic_post_id. It holds 30 Meta and TikTok posts so far (plus 7 LinkedIn).

  • Meta: our ad-name rule finds 21 of 21 tagged posts (19 are tracked campaign posts). The rule is safe to use for the history before September.
  • TikTok: the Spark item-id link finds 6 of 9, but 0 of the 3 tracked campaign posts. Those 3 boosts were invisible before the tag. Put the tag first.

Method

How we kept the numbers honest

  • Truth for organic: Instagram opt-in (private) views. Before a boost they equal public views (typical ratio 1.001), so they are a fair organic truth.
  • No fitting on the test posts: the organic curve comes from unboosted posts; the methods with a fitted factor use leave-one-out (the post itself is never in its own fit).
  • Paid totals by placement come from the EDW Meta ad tables. Andrew's unified table is built from them (its sums match), so every tier uses the same definitions.
  • One read per post for post-level errors (latest read at least 2 days after the last ad day). Campaign errors use 2,000 random 10-post groups.
  • No paid data in model inputs. No paid date, boost flag, ad name, spend, opt-in or SocAPI field. detector/features.py asserts this.
  • Locked test by time: train before 2026-07-01, test after; scored once. Model family (13 candidates) and threshold chosen on train only, with 5-fold CV grouped by creator.
  • Re-run check: the test scores were re-created from code for this report and match the first run to 2×10⁻¹⁶. Nothing was tuned after the test was seen.
  • Labels are not perfect. TikTok "organic" means no VN ad link. Andrew's tag already shows one wrong "organic" label, so test precision is a floor.
